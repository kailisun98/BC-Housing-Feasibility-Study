# What they wish the tool had, 23 Nov 2026

**Simulated wish lists (role-plays by AI reviewers), not practitioners' statements.** The same four
reviewers who tested the tool were asked, at the same time and separately, what features they
hoped for and which steps of their real work the flows do not carry. Bugs were excluded; this is
what is absent. Each answer is condensed here; S / M / L is the reviewer's own estimate of how
much of their week it would change.

## Asked for by more than one seat

| What | Who asked | In their words |
|---|---|---|
| **Say what kind of housing it is, first.** A switch (independent / supportive / transitional / shelter) that sets the unit types, the rules that apply and the funders shown. | Covenant House manager (ranked first, L), funder reader (L), First United manager | "Without it the tool is answering questions about a different building from mine." |
| **A program finder.** From who is housed (rent tiers, supportive count, Indigenous-led operator) to the program family, with intake status and "not held in this tool" where true. | both managers (L), funder reader (L) | "It would have spared three of the five memos a false 'does not meet'." |
| **An operating page.** Rents by population, running cost, support staffing, debt service, ending in the subsidy needed per home per year. | both managers (L, M), funder reader (L) | "That last figure is what I compare across submissions." |
| **Named capital sources with a status** (committed / applied / assumed), land shown as the owner's contribution, municipal waivers; a separate budget for community floors with "the society must raise $X". | both managers (M, L), funder reader (M) | "The showcase carries church land at $0 and hides the proponent's best card." |
| **A front sheet shaped as an ask.** Who is housed, who operates, which program door, the pre-development amount and what it buys, the top three risks. | both managers (M, S), funder reader (the one addition asked for), architect (M) | "Page one is close; no ask, no amount, no risks." |
| **A pre-application enquiry sheet for the City**, made from the "not assessed" and open items, and a place to record what staff said, dated, which the next run reads. | architect, both managers, funder reader | "City back to me: a place to record what staff said, which then replaces the benchmark." |

| **Roles in a partnership**: land owner, owner of the homes, operator, and the instrument between them; the operator supplying the rents and running costs. | First United manager (M), funder reader, Covenant House manager | "Operator into the memo: no flow asks them." |

## The architect (modelled on Public Architecture)

Real workflow against the tool: pin the site (partly: one parcel, not an assembly), read what
governs it (partly), find precedents (missing), test two or three forms (partly: one bar), check
what kills a form, shadow, daylight, separation, parkade (missing), area schedule (partly),
cost and funder fit (covered as arithmetic), one page to the client (partly).

1. A plate that follows the lot: L, cranked bar, courtyard as options. **L**
2. Site assembly: several addresses merged into one site, dedications taken off. **M**
3. A precedent table for the block. **L**
4. Area schedule export (CSV): homes by type and floor, net, gross, efficiency. **M**
5. Per-storey heights and uses (a 4.5 m shop floor, amenity on the roof). **S**
6. A unit-size profile switch: BC Housing's, or a set typed in. **M**
7. Neighbour-aware yards: what the site adjoins, whether there is a lane. **S**
8. A one-page option comparison to print on Friday. **M**

Missing hand-offs: to the City (enquiry sheet) and back (what staff said); from the manager (the
society's program brief as an input, not a default mix); to consultants (open items split by who
must be hired: surveyor, arborist, code, cost); from the funder (the intake calendar and which
sheet a submission needs).

Would not want: a model that designs plans or writes findings; rendered drawings ("the more
finished the sheets look, the more a client believes them"); auto-filled answers without a
visible "unconfirmed" mark.

## The development manager (modelled on Covenant House Vancouver)

Real workflow against the tool: confirm the site (covered), decide what kind of housing and who
operates (missing), call BC Housing and the City's housing planner (missing), size the building
with the architect (partly: no program floors), build the capital stack (partly: three lump
fields), build the operating budget with the program director (missing), work out neighbourhood
and Council risk (missing), write the board paper (partly: not shaped as an ask).

1. Housing-type switch. **L**
2. Operating page: staffing per shift, supports, rent by population, subsidy a year. **L**
3. A funder library for the sector, each stream marked open, closed or by invitation. **L**
4. Named capital sources with a status. **M**
5. Beds and rooms as a unit type, plus program floors. **M**
6. A studio-led "supportive" candidate in the suggestions. **M**
7. Board paper export: the ask. **M**
8. Precedent comparison with a built public case. **S**

Missing hand-offs: to the operator (staffing confirmed, an operating figure returned); to the
funder; to the City; the board's decision, conditions and date recorded against the revision;
what the pre-development money is spent on and in what order.

Would not want: generated text in the memo; community-opposition scores; more mandatory inputs
before a first answer ("not sure must stay a normal answer").

## The development manager (modelled on First United)

Real workflow against the tool: decide whether to rebuild while services keep running (missing),
confirm what we own and what constrains it (covered), find a housing partner and agree who owns,
leases and operates what (missing), ask the City which approval route applies (partly), match the
people housed to the funding streams (partly), split the budget between homes and community
floors (partly), check the operating side (missing), take two pages to the board (partly).

1. A program finder that works from who is housed. **L**
2. A two-part budget: housing and community space, with what the society must raise. **L**
3. Partnership roles, each generating open items. **M**
4. Approval route as a question: development permit under an official development plan, rezoning, not sure. **M**
5. A named contacts column: organisation, role and the first document to request. **M**
6. An operating screen for supportive housing. **M**
7. A board cover page. **S**
8. Area plan and sub-area set from the address, with the plan's housing requirements. **S**

Missing hand-offs: a version the partner society can mark up and return; the City enquiry; the
funder's officer; the board's vote against the revision; the service-continuity plan (temporary
sites, timing) as an input the architect and the budget both see.

Would not want: guessed funder amounts or award likelihoods; a model writing the verdicts; more
drawings in the manager's memo.

## The funder-side reader (modelled on BC Housing)

`docs/user-flows.md` gives this reader one role, reading the PDF. The real exchange has several
returns: the society's first email (missing: no memo says whom the homes are for), which program
door (partly), site and planning sanity (covered), operator and who pays to run it (missing),
order-of-magnitude budget and the ask (partly), pre-development support (partly), the
municipality's position and a schedule to a construction start (missing), entering a stream or
waiting (missing: nothing says what to hold ready while no intake is open).

1. A project identity block: population, tenure, operator, landowner's contribution, the ask. **L**
2. A program router. **L**
3. An operating page. **L**
4. A readiness timeline: rezoning, permit and start as dates against the program's start requirement. **M**
5. A pre-development budget. **M**
6. A contributions ledger, each line confirmed or sought. **M**
7. A "tested / not tested" box per program. **M**
8. A "what changed since you last saw this" page written for the funder. **S**

Missing hand-offs: funder back to proponent; operator into the memo; the municipality's response
with its date; the site-control document itself, attached and dated, instead of a dropdown; a
"hold" state between calls (what stays valid, what expires, when to rerun).

Would not want: a score or ranking against rated criteria ("it would read as predicting an
evaluation"); any funder's name, logo or template styling; generated narrative on need or
operator capacity.

## What none of them wants

All four said the same thing unprompted: no model writing findings or verdicts inside the memo.
They trust it because nothing in it is guessing or persuading.
