Will this housing scheme be allowed, and will a funder back it?

First Read checks a site and a building option against Vancouver's zoning, the Province's rules, City policy, design standards and the funding programs, all at once, before the first drawing. It writes a short memo with three answers and shows every rule it used.

The problem: the rules are read late, by different people.

A board votes pre-development money, often $200,000 to $500,000, on a feasibility read that cites nothing and carries no date. The rules it rests on sit in five layers, each read by someone else at a later stage. The memo reads all five on day one, and dates what it read.

How it works.

  1. 1 Give it a site and an option

    The site: address, zoning, lot size, trees, owner. The option: storeys, floor area, units, rents, funders. "Not sure" is an honest answer: a blank comes back as uncertain, never as a guess.

  2. 2 Run the rules

    As of 23 Oct 2026, 17 dated, cited rules from 13 public sources, in the five layers above. Each memo on a site is a revision, R1, R2 and so on, and the same inputs under the same rules always give the same memo.

  3. 3 Read the memo

    1. Is it allowed? Outright, or by rezoning.
    2. Does it fit the funders? Yes, no, or what is missing.
    3. What is still unsure? Each open item, with a named person.

    Beside the answers it draws the massing the rules allow, in plan, section and 3D, and a pyRevit script rebuilds it in Revit on an annotated sheet.

Change a number and run again to compare the two runs side by side.

Every finding shows its working.

One row of the memo: the clause and its version, the status in a word, what the option proposes against what the clause permits, the ask between them, and the person who resolves it.
