Who it's for

One memo,
three kinds of reader.

Housing societies, co-ops, churches and First Nations build homes that rent below market, and they have no development profit to absorb a wrong guess. The people working on one of their sites each read different documents at different times. The memo gives them one document they all read.

02What each reader does

Each reader takes a different path through the same tool.

A switch in the tool's top bar (Manager, Architect, Reader) changes the layout for each one. The manager walks a route of three stops, one card at a time; the architect gets the site and option schedules, the drawings and the ledger open with the Revit export at hand; the reader sees the memo alone, as on paper. The filled step in each path is the one that reader came for.

Flow 1

The development manager

Starts from a site and leaves with a memo, before any money goes to design.

Open the tool as the manager
  1. 1
    Types an address. The tool finds it in the City's open data, takes the zoning district and the parcel from the City's records, and draws the parcel on a map to check before it is used.01 The site · Look up · Is this the parcel?
  2. 2
    Answers what the data could not tell, once. The policy areas, water and sewer, the owner, whether the buildings stay, which street of a corner lot is the front. "Not sure" is an answer, and the memo reports that rule as uncertain.01 The site · the schedule of questions
  3. 3
    Shapes the option from the rules, or from their own figures. The programs as toggles, what the site allows, then a candidate: funder-ready, outright or the staff benchmark, with four figures to change in place and every other figure behind "All 21 figures", each with its rule beside it.   4. 4

03What stays with people

Six calls stay with people.

An answer from a chatbot can't be cited, repeated or dated. The memo's findings can, and the memo leaves each judgement to the person it belongs to.

  1. 01Site facts

    Confirming what is true of the lot.

    A person reads the parcel's transit-oriented tier off the City's map and enters it.

  2. 02The option

    Choosing what to test.

    The storeys, unit mix, tenure and which program to go after are the housing provider's call.

  3. 03The massing

    Judging the picture.

    The massing shows what the rules allow. Where it stands on the lot, which street is the front and how the floors are laid out are the architect's to draw, and whether it would make a good building is theirs to judge.

  4. 04Uncertain rules

    Interpreting what the engine cannot.

    A CD-1 negotiation, a waived charge or a height above the staff benchmark is named in the memo and handed to a person.

  5. 05The decision

    Whether to proceed.

    The board votes, with the memo in front of it.

  6. 06The stamp

    Anything that gets stamped.

    A person checks the rule table before it becomes data, and the numbers before a revision is issued.

04Who tests it

Each reader judges the part they would use.

User sessions run through November and December 2026 under the course brief's rules: nobody's site or documents are used without permission, nothing confidential goes into the public repository, and people are credited only with their consent.

  1. By 13 NovSession 1

    A non-profit's development manager

    Runs the tool on their own site and reacts to the massing and the memo as a board would.

  2. By 27 NovSession 2

    A project architect at a housing firm

    Uses the memo for a pre-application or a Development Permit Enquiry and compares it with a recent feasibility package.

  3. By 3 DecSession 3

  4. BetweenAsynchronous

  5. 6 and 27 NovPeer reviews

    Reads the memo without a briefing and points out what a non-expert can't follow.

    Supplies housing-needs figures for the test municipality and comments on the rule table.
