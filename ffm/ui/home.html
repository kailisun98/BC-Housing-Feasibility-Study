The problem: the rules are read late, by different people.

A board votes pre-development money, often $200,000 to $500,000, on a feasibility read that cites nothing and carries no date.

The rules it rests on sit in five layers, each read by someone else at a later stage, so the first drawing usually comes before the first rule check. The memo is that check, done on day one and dated.

How it works: three steps.

Give it a site and an option, run the rules, read the memo. Same inputs and the same rules always give the same memo.

What the memo answers.

Three questions, in order, each answered with a status word. Every finding shows the clause it comes from and the numbers it used.

1 Is it allowed?

Outright, or by rezoning.

Density and height against the district schedule, the rezoning policy that applies, the Province's transit rules, the family-unit and tree by-laws.

2 Does it fit the funders?

Yes, no, or what is missing.

The Community Housing Fund, BC Builds and the others, against their published criteria: proponent, rent mix, accessible units, site control, lease.

3 What is still unsure?

Each open item, with a named person.

The architect, the development manager or the planner, and the document they need to check.
