The case

998 E 19th Ave.
Six storeys on church land.

Council approved this scheme on 25 July 2024. Its inputs come from the City's public referral report and the Shape Your City page, and the few figures those don't give are labelled as placeholders. The tool arrives at the City's own numbers: a floor space ratio of 2.95 against 1.20 allowed outright, 36% family units against a 35% minimum, and 20 trees required against the 17 the plan leaves.

01Page one

What a board member reads first.

Page one has the title block, the four answers with the rules that decide them, and the open items with the person who resolves each. This is the site's first memo, R1, under the rules in force on 23 October 2026.

Site998 E 19th Ave, Vancouver · RM-1 · 2,811 m²
OptionAs approved by Council, 25 Jul 2024 · 6 storeys · 22.3 m · 105 units
RevisionR1 · rules as of 23 Oct 2026
Rezoning pathCD-1 under the Interim Rezoning Policy for Social Housing

1 · Is it allowed?

Not outright: rezoning required. By rezoning: yes, with conditions.

RM-1 zoning allows an FSR of 1.20 and a height of 10.7 m outright; the scheme proposes 2.95 and 22.3 m. As 100% social housing it is eligible for rezoning under the Interim Rezoning Policy, but its height is above the staff benchmark of 19.9 to 21.3 m and will be negotiated.

2 · Does it fit the funders?

Not yet confirmed.

BC Builds needs to know how many units rent at least 20% below market. The Community Housing Fund needs the split across rent tiers, the number of accessible units and the land tenure. Its 2025 call for proposals was cancelled in February 2026; on 22 Sep 2026 its page said BC Housing will post when it next seeks proposals. CMHC's Affordable Housing Fund takes applications continuously.

3 · What is still unsure?

Seven open items, each with a named person.

Two conditions attach to the rezoning and two figures are missing for the funders. No yard rule checked the setbacks, a church site needs its heritage status confirmed, and the plate the setbacks leave holds 67 of the 105 homes. Each has a person to settle it.

4 · Does the money work?

Needs more sources. The gap is $12.5 million, 23% of the capital cost.

A first-pass pro‑forma from the dated, cited assumptions of layer F and the fund's provisional grant, against a 15% feasibility bar. Every line names its rule and its source; the placeholders (soft costs, contingency, the operating figures) are marked as such.

The last row, the homes on the plate, reads the tool's own first layout, drawn from the option's figures: rough plans to test the numbers, not the plans Council approved. Its exits pass on every floor.

02One row of the memo

Every finding shows its working.

Each row gives the clause and its version, the status in a word, what the option proposes next to what the clause permits, what it would take to close the gap ("the ask"), and the person who resolves it.

03Revisions

Three memos on one site, each numbered and dated.

Change the option and the answers change. Change the date and a rule may move to a new version, and the memo says which. When two memos are compared, both revision numbers are shown, the way two issues of a drawing are.

04A second case

1010 Expo Blvd, a tower on a CD-1 site.

The site is a triangular parking lot between Expo Blvd, Pacific Blvd and the Cambie Bridge, in the False Creek North plan area, zoned CD-1 (324). A 29-storey social housing tower got its development permit in February 2025 and is under construction, due in 2029. The inputs come from public City pages; the by-law's limits were read from the City's PDF of CD-1 (324) and matched to the site by its by-law number.

  1. R1 · 23 Oct 2026As permitted

    29 storeys, 23,215 m² at FSR 5.94.

    Outright: yes By rezoning: not yet confirmed Funders: not yet confirmed

    The by-law fixes the residential floor area by sub-area, and the tower's 23,215 m² is within sub-area B's 23,600 m²; its 88.45 m is under the 91 m the by-law allows. Seven open items remain: the yards, the transit-oriented tier, the family mix, the social housing definition, the trees and the two funders' figures. With no rents given, the gap is the whole capital cost until the manager enters them.

    Twelve storeys with the full funder data.

    Outright: yes By rezoning: not yet confirmed Funders: not yet confirmed

    A hypothetical option, well inside the by-law. Five open items remain, because a person still has to settle the yards, the parcel's transit-oriented tier, the trees, the fund's figures and the homes the plate holds (100 of 120); the gap is 44% of the capital cost.

05A third case

375 Glen Drive, a CD-1 read from its referral report.

A corner lot at Glen Drive and E Hastings St in the Downtown Eastside, rezoned from M-1 to CD-1 (819) as 1015 East Hastings: a 15‑storey wing of social housing on the lane and a 13‑storey wing of secured market rental on Glen Drive, 163 homes and an 80-bed shelter, from the City's referral report of January 2021. The enacted by-law is not in the public PDFs the tool could reach, so its limits were read from the report's draft provisions and the memo says they are unverified.

  1. R1 · 17 Nov 2026Before the by-law was in the rules

    The same option, with no CD-1 (819) rules.

    Outright: not assessed By rezoning: no Funders: no

    The memo says the by-law is not in the rules and names which one, instead of calling the towers permitted. The rezoning path fails on the city-wide family-unit rule, which the Downtown Eastside Plan replaces with its own target.

    Open R1 ↗
  2. R2 · 17 Nov 2026As referred to public hearing

    14,729 m² at FSR 6.90, 51 m: within the draft by-law.

    Outright: yes By rezoning: not yet confirmed Funders: no

    The floor area and the height pass against the draft provisions, with one open item saying the figures are unverified until the enacted by-law is read. The funders' answer is no: the rent tiers as entered (55% market, 23% rent-geared-to-income, 23% deep subsidy) miss the Community Housing Fund's 30 / 50 / 20 mix. Five open items; the gap is 47% of the capital cost.
