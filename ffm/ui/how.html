How it works

An address and an option go in.
A dated, cited memo comes out.

The rules are stored as data with a source and a date on every line, and ordinary code applies them, so the same inputs always give the same answer. A person checks the rule table before it is used and reviews the memo before anything is decided. A language model can draft a rule from a PDF, but it never runs inside the engine.

01Inside the tool

Five inputs are checked against six layers of rules to produce six outputs.

Each rule is stored as a structured clause with its source, its section and the date it took effect. Once it is in, the tool reuses it for every address, and someone reads the source again only when the rule changes.

02The logic

Code applies the rules. People make the calls.

There are twelve steps. One takes the inputs and five are ordinary code; one uses a language model, once, to turn a PDF into a clause and a date; three are checks that stop the run if they fail; and two belong to a person. If an input is marked "not sure", the rule that needs it stops there and the memo reports it as uncertain instead of passing it.

03The rule stack

Six layers apply to one address at the same time.

Each layer comes from a different body and changes on its own schedule. Where two layers conflict on geometry, the stricter one governs, and where the Province sets a minimum, it overrides a lower local limit.

  1. A · 62 rulesZoning district

    The shape a building may take.

    Height, yards, floor space ratio and parking, from Vancouver's Zoning and Development By-law. Twenty-one districts are read from the City's own PDFs: RM-3, RM-3A, RM-4, the RM-5 family, RT-3, R1-1, the C-2 family and the RR rental districts. A CD-1 site, zoned by its own by-law, matches the rules keyed to its by-law number.

  2. B · 2 rulesProvince

    Minimums a city cannot go below.

    The least number of homes allowed on a small lot, set by its size and its distance to a bus stop, and minimum heights near transit stations. Bills 44 and 47, in force since 2024.

  3. C · 6 rulesCity policy

    What attaches to a rezoning.

    Family-sized units, the definition of social housing, below-market rents, the tree by-law and development charges, and the Downtown Eastside Plan's own family target where a site is in that area. The family-unit rule changed on 27 Oct 2026.

  4. D · 3 rulesDesign standards

    What shapes the option itself.

    BC Housing's minimum unit areas, the building code basis under the 2025 Vancouver Building By-law, and the code's exit clauses: how many exits a floor needs, how far apart, how far a unit's door is from one, and how long a corridor may run past the last. The exits are read on every floor of the plans; the rest are flagged for the architect.

  5. E · 4 rulesFunders

    Whether the money comes.

    The published criteria of the Community Housing Fund, BC Builds, the pre‑development loan and CMHC's readiness test, applied as written. They aren't law, but they decide whether a project gets funded.

  6. F · 7 assumptionsAssumptions

    What the pro‑forma counts with.

    An early-stage (Class D) construction cost, the Statistics Canada price index, the Bank of Canada rate, CMHC rents and CMHC's coverage ratio. Each is dated and cited, one command refreshes them, and any placeholder is labelled as one.

  7. By designLeft to a person

    Four things stay uncertain.

    Which transit-oriented tier a parcel falls in, what a CD-1 rezoning is negotiated to, whether council waives the charges, and the land price on a private site. The memo names each one and hands it to a person.

What it does not know yet

  • Five of the 144 rules are entered but not yet checked against their documents: CD-1 (819), read from a referral report's draft; the building code's exit clauses; the transit-oriented tiers 4 and 5; and the Downtown Eastside family target. A district schedule like that becomes an open item in the memo of any site it is used on.
  • RM-1's figures, the approved case's own district, come from the City's referral report rather than the district schedule.
  • A district with no schedule in the rules reads "not assessed", never "yes".
  • In the costs, the soft costs, the contingency and the operating figures are placeholders until a project gives its own, and the construction cost is a figure for two- to four-unit buildings.

04What you receive

Three stops in the tool, then a memo to keep.

You give the tool a site, shape an option and run it. What comes back is a dated memo with four answers, the open items with a name on each, the drawings and a PDF to hand round.

1 The site 2 The option 3 Run 4 The memo
  1. 01The memo

    One dated revision, R1, R2, R3.

    Its title block names the site, the option and the date of the rules it read. Two plain pages come first; every clause, with its numbers and its source, follows.

  2. 02Four answers

    Allowed, funded, unsure, money.

    Is it allowed, outright and by rezoning; does it fit the funders; what is still unsure; does the money work. Each answer names the rules that decide it.

  3. 03Open items

    A name on every one.

    Each open item says who settles it and what to do next. The approved case has eight; the diagram below puts them in order, person by person.

  4. 04Drawings and a PDF

    The massing, the plans, the paper.

    The massing the rules allow in plan, section and 3D, the floor plans with their exits checked, and a Revit sheet from the same numbers. Download PDF lays it all out for a board.
