Label the uncertain parcels

40 parcels the classifier (ffm/parcel_shapes.py) is least sure of, from the City's parcel polygons (Open Government Licence – Vancouver), every one a taxable lot with an address (since 12 Nov 2026 parks, rights-of-way, strata plans and untaxed remnants are kept out; the grey line under each address gives the zoning at the parcel's point and what the tax roll says). North is up in every thumbnail; beside it the parcel is drawn over the real site (OpenStreetMap, or aerial imagery with the switch above; the maps load as you scroll). On the map, click the edge that fronts the street: the whole edge between two white corners turns red (a curved frontage is one edge), and a second edge marks the cross street on a corner lot (amber). Then tick the shape you would call it; a note helps when none of the words fit, or when the parcel is not what the City draws (a consolidated site, a road closure). The draft stays in this browser until you download it, and only in this browser: open the page in your own (double-click label.html), not in a preview pane that forgets its storage when it closes, and download labels.json as you go. The classes: rectangle (four corners, square-ish), near rectangle (a bevelled corner, a slight skew, a short jog), trapezoid (four sides, not square-cornered), L shape (six corners, one inward), flag lot (a narrow strip leads to a wider body), irregular (anything else: triangles, curves, many sides), multi part (more than one piece).
