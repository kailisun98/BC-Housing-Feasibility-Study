Who it's for

One memo,
three kinds of reader.

Housing societies, co-ops, churches and First Nations build homes that rent below market, and they have no development profit to absorb a wrong guess. The people working on one of their sites each read different documents at different times. The memo gives them one document they all read.

On a board, or reading for a funder or the City? Start with Flow 3: the front sheet, the open items and a decision.

02What each reader does

Each reader takes a different path through the same tool.

A switch in the tool's top bar, Manager, Architect, Reader or Operator, lays the page out for each of them; the reader sees the memo alone, as on paper, and the operator who will run the building sees its operating figures first. The filled step in each path is the one that person came for.

Flow 1

The development manager

Starts from a site and leaves with a memo, before any money goes to design.

Open the tool as the manager
  1. 1
    Types an address. The tool finds the parcel and its zoning in the City's open data and shows it on a map to confirm.01 The site
  2. 2
    Answers what the data could not tell, once. "Not sure" is an answer, and the memo reports that rule as uncertain.01 The site · questions
  3. 3
    Shapes the option, from the rules (funder-ready, outright or the staff benchmark) or from their own figures, each figure with its rule beside it.02 The option
  4. 4
    Enters the money and runs the rules. Land, equity and other grants; four answers and the drawings come back in under a second.03 Run
  5. 5
    Works the open items. Each names who resolves it and what to do.answer 3
  6. 6
    Issues it to the architect, with a note, and takes the PDF to the board.Issue · Download PDF

Flow 3

The board, the planner, the funder

Starts from the PDF and ends with a decision, without a briefing.

Open the memo as a reader
  1. 1
    Reads the front sheet without a briefing: the address, option, revision and rules date, the ask (who is housed, who operates, the amount, the decision wanted), then the four answers with the rules that decide them.PDF · the ask and the answers
  2. 2
    Reads the open items, each with a person. A planner looks for the uncertain rules, and a funder's officer for the missing figures. The short PDF stops here, with the money and the sources.PDF · answer 3
  3. 3
    Follows a reference. Every rule the memo reads has a reference, its layer's letter and its place in that memo's ledger (A1, C5, E2), the same on screen and on paper, and every source has a number and a link.PDF · clauses, sources
  4. 4
    Decides whether to vote the pre‑development money. Because the memo is dated, a later reader can tell whether it still holds.the board gate

Flow 4

The operator

Starts from the memo the manager sends with "can you run this?" and leaves with the operating figures signed as its own.

Open the tool as the operator
  1. 1
    Opens the run the manager issued, on the operator's desk: what it takes to run the building for the years after it opens.issued to you · the operator's figures
  2. 2
    Enters the building's own figures and signs them: rents by tier, vacancy, the running cost a home and a bed, support staffing, the operating subsidy and its term.Sign these figures
  3. 3
    Checks the plans for running it: the area schedule, the elevators, a desk that sees the entry, laundry, the services room and the accessible homes.The spaces it runs on
  4. 4
    Runs again and issues it back. The memo then says the operating figures are signed, and by whom.Run again · Issue

03What stays with people

Six calls stay with people.

An answer from a chatbot can't be cited, repeated or dated. The memo's findings can, and the memo leaves each judgement to the person it belongs to.

  1. 01Site facts

    Confirming what is true of the lot.

    A person reads the parcel's transit-oriented tier off the City's map and enters it.

  2. 02The option

    Choosing what to test.

    The storeys, unit mix, tenure and which program to go after are the housing provider's call.

  3. 03The massing

    Judging the picture.

    The massing shows what the rules allow. Where it stands on the lot, which street is the front and how the floors are laid out are the architect's to draw, and whether it would make a good building is theirs to judge.

  4. 04Uncertain rules

    Interpreting what the engine cannot.

    A CD-1 negotiation, a waived charge or a height above the staff benchmark is named in the memo and handed to a person.

  5. 05The decision

    Whether to proceed.

    The board votes, with the memo in front of it.

  6. 06The stamp

    Anything that gets stamped.

    A person checks the rule table before it becomes data, and the numbers before a revision is issued.

04Who tests it

Each reader judges the part they would use.

User sessions are scheduled across November and December 2026, on the dates below, under the course brief's rules: nobody's site or documents are used without permission, nothing confidential goes into the public repository, and people are credited only with their consent.

  1. 13 NovSession 1

    Runs the tool on their own site and reacts to the massing and the memo as a board would.

  2. 27 NovSession 2

  3. 3 DecSession 3

    Reads the memo without a briefing and points out what a non-expert can't follow.

    Supplies housing-needs figures for the test municipality and comments on the rule table.
